WHAT MAKES AN EXPERIENCE ACTUALLY IMMERSIVE

Not the headset. Not the AR layer. A working theory, illustrated with seven things we've actually built.

Category
Perspective
Read time
6 min
Format
Article + coverflow
Published
EDT Blog

"Immersive" gets used as a synonym for "has a headset" often enough that it's started to lose the word it actually means. A VR build with a confusing menu isn't immersive. An AR filter nobody understood how to open isn't immersive. The technology was never the part that mattered.

What actually makes something immersive is simpler and harder: the person using it stops thinking about the interface and starts thinking about the thing the interface is showing them. That's the whole test. Everything below is a different answer to the same brief — how do you make the interface disappear.

Look back at the shelf above and the technology is almost incidental — a phone camera, a headset, a wall of sensors, a browser tab. None of them are the point. What's consistent is that every one of those builds gave the person something to do that mattered more than the tech doing it: complete a scenario, find a story on a wall, see a shoe on their own feet, watch a room respond to them personally.

That's the actual brief behind "make it immersive," every time someone asks for it. Not more technology. A reason to stop noticing there's any technology there at all.
